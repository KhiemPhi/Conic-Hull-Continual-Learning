  dataset       T         seed-only        structural  struct-seed  all seeds>0

  VERDICT: on A-Last the diversity design does NOT beat seed-only ensembling
  (+0.08, sign flips across seeds). On A-Avg it wins by a small but perfectly

  and individually WEAKER (m32 79.35, q32b70 79.68 vs seed-variants 80.53-80.80)
  yet ensemble to the same place -- a diversity/strength tradeoff netting ~zero.
  So the ensemble gain over the best single member is GENERIC ENSEMBLING; the
  member-design choice is an ablation that did not matter. The cone still works
  on a seed-only ensemble (cone-sub +0.30 vs +0.27 structural), i.e. the read-out
  benefit is INDEPENDENT of how the members were diversified.

  C6 EQUAL-INFERENCE-BUDGET BASELINE -- ONE first-session LoRA r32 on ViT-L/16,
  same recipe, same PILOT order, same RanPAC read-out. C7 measured 5x ViT-B/16 at
  0.2381s vs 1x ViT-L/16 at 0.0724s for one batch, so the 5-member ensemble costs
  3.3x a single ViT-L at inference.
  dataset       T  n    ViT-L A-Last     ViT-L A-Avg   ours(ViT-B)         vs ours

  VERDICT: a SINGLE ViT-L beats the whole 5-member ViT-B ensemble by +2.26 A-Last
  / +1.91 A-Avg at 1/3.3 the inference cost. The efficiency argument for the
  ensemble is therefore DEAD, and 'why not just use a bigger backbone?' has a
  decisive answer. NOTE this is OFF-PROTOCOL: every published PTM-CIL baseline
  (GR-LoRA, MACIL, RanPAC, ADAM) is ViT-B/16-IN21k, so the GR-LoRA comparison above
  remains matched-backbone and valid. The honest reading is that the entire ViT-B
  PTM-CIL literature, ours included, is dominated by scaling the backbone -- which
  is a finding worth reporting rather than hiding.
