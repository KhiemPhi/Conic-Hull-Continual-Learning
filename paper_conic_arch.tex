\title{\bf From Conic Hulls to $k$-Means Hulls:\\
Read-Out Geometry for First-Session Class-Incremental Learning}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
First-session adaptation (FSA) freezes a pre-trained backbone after one task and
reads it with a light-weight classifier. We ask how much the \emph{read-out} can
extract, using conic hulls as the vehicle. We show that (i) the classical conic
hull, whose generators are extremal rays recovered by the Successive Projection
Algorithm (SPA), is worse than choosing generators uniformly at random; (ii)
replacing SPA with $k$-means centroids---formally, moving from a separable to a
convex non-negative factorisation---is worth up to $61.8$ points and is the
single largest design choice in the family; (iii) the resulting object is still a
conic hull, but of a learned dictionary rather than of the class, which forfeits
containment while preserving non-negativity; and (iv) augmenting the generator
set along the principal directions of \emph{within-class} variation gives a
further $+0.28$ at matched storage. Composed with a first-session baseline
(A\textsuperscript{+}) that stores no per-class covariance, the read-out reaches
$80.05$ on ImageNet-R ($T{=}10$) against a RanPAC bar of $80.28$. We report the
negative results in full: the metric, not the cone, carries $\sim$85\% of the
gain; a random-feature lift---worth $+9.26$ to a ridge---\emph{hurts} an angular
read-out because it doubles mean pairwise cosine; and four of the five scoring
rules in common use are provably the same rule.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

A pre-trained encoder $\phi:\mathcal{X}\to\R^{d}$ ($d{=}768$, ViT-B/16) is adapted
with LoRA on the first task only and then frozen; all later tasks contribute
features and statistics but no gradients. Write $\bx = \phi(\cdot)/\lVert\phi(\cdot)\rVert
\in S^{d-1}$. For each class $c$ the read-out stores a small set of vectors and
produces a score $s_c(\bq)$; prediction is $\arg\max_c s_c(\bq)$.

Because the backbone is frozen, per-class objects fitted at a class's birth stage
remain valid for the rest of the run, and final-stage accuracy over all classes
\emph{is} A-Last exactly. This makes read-out ablations roughly $10\times$ cheaper
than a full staged replay.

\section{The conic hull as a read-out}

\subsection{Object and score}
Given generators $\bV=[\bv_1,\dots,\bv_R]^{\!\top}$, the conic hull is
\begin{equation}
C(\bV) = \Big\{\textstyle\sum_r \lambda_r \bv_r : \lambda_r \ge 0\Big\}
       = \{\bV^{\!\top}\bw : \bw \ge 0\}.
\end{equation}
The score is the cosine to the nearest point of the cone. For unit $\bq$,
Moreau's decomposition gives $\langle \bq, \Pi_C\bq\rangle = \lVert\Pi_C\bq\rVert^2$,
hence
\begin{equation}
s(\bq) \;=\; \cos\angle\!\big(\bq,\Pi_C\bq\big) \;=\; \lVert \Pi_C \bq \rVert ,
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
the exact analogue of a subspace read-out's $\lVert P\bq\rVert$, with the
projection taken onto a cone rather than a subspace.

\paragraph{Four scoring rules are one rule.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:score} implies $\lVert\text{rec}\rVert = \cos$, so the
``residual coverage'' score $\cos\cdot\lVert\text{rec}\rVert$ equals $\cos^2$;
the ``angular'' score $1-\tfrac{2}{\pi}\arccos\cos$ and the ``NNLS residual''
score $1-\tfrac12\sqrt{2-2\cos}$ are likewise monotone in $\cos$. All four
therefore induce \emph{identical} rankings (identical AUROC, identical
$\arg\max$). Treating them as distinct arms, as is common, measures the same
quantity repeatedly.

\paragraph{Nesting.} Restricting $\bw$ to its $k$ largest-cosine coordinates gives
$C_1\subseteq C_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq C_R$, and $s_k$ is nondecreasing in $k$.
At $k{=}1$ the score reduces exactly to $\max_r\langle\bq,\bv_r\rangle$: \emph{the
conic hull is a one-parameter generalisation of multi-prototype matching}, with
multi-prototype as its degenerate member.

\subsection{Where the generators come from}
Both classical choices are the same constrained factorisation with different
solutions. Write the dictionary as $\bV = \mathbf{G}\bX$ with
$\mathbf{G}\ge 0$, $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{SPA / separable NMF.} $\mathbf{G}$ restricted to \emph{vertices} of
the simplex, $\mathbf{g}_r = \mathbf{e}_{i_r}$: each generator \emph{is} a data
point, selected greedily by maximum residual norm (equivalently, QR with column
pivoting). Under separability this provably recovers the true extremal rays.
\item \textbf{$k$-means / convex NMF.} $\mathbf{g}_r$ the uniform distribution on
a cluster: each generator is a \emph{convex combination} of data points.
\end{itemize}
So SPA sits at a vertex of the constraint set and $k$-means at a barycentre. They
optimise opposite things: outward extent versus central representativeness.

\paragraph{Consequences.} A centroid averages $n/R$ samples and has variance
$\sigma^2R/n$; an SPA ray is a single sample---and specifically the
\emph{argmax} over $n$ residual norms, so its noise lies in the upper tail. With
$n{\approx}100$ and $R{=}4$ the variance ratio exceeds $25\times$ before the
selection bias.

Empirically (Table~\ref{tab:gens}) SPA is not merely suboptimal: it is worse than
selecting $R$ data points \emph{uniformly at random}, by 19 points.

\subsection{What ``$k$-means hull'' means precisely}
\label{sec:naming}
The object is still a conic hull---$C(\bV)$ is closed under positive scaling,
convex, polyhedral, and pointed (all pairwise cosines are positive, so
$\sum_r\bv_r$ strictly separates)---but it is the conic hull \emph{of the
dictionary}, not of the class:
\begin{equation}
C(\bV_{k\text{-means}}) \;\subsetneq\; \conv\text{-}\cone(\bX), \qquad
\bX \not\subseteq C(\bV_{k\text{-means}}).
\end{equation}
Non-negativity of the coefficients is preserved; \emph{containment} of the class
is not, and neither is the separability recovery guarantee. We recommend the
phrase ``a polyhedral cone spanned by $R$ learned generators''; ``conic hull''
unqualified will be read as $\cone(\bX)$ and imports containment intuitions that
no longer hold.

Containment is also the wrong target. Adding a generator can only enlarge the
cone, so a containment-optimal generator set maximises coverage---precisely the
property that destroys discrimination when the score is a distance to that set.
SPA optimises coverage; $k$-means does not.

\section{The metric carries most of the gain}

Let $\Sigma$ be the pooled within-class scatter, which is \emph{additive across
classes} and therefore accumulates task by task with no stored samples, exactly
as RanPAC's Gram matrix does. With $\bW = \operatorname{chol}(\Sigma^{-1})$ we
fit and score in $\bx \mapsto \bW^{\!\top}\bx/\lVert\cdot\rVert$.

Generators are built in the birth-time whitened space, mapped back via
$\bW_{\text{birth}}^{-1}$, and stored there; at any later stage they are
re-metricated as $\widehat{\bV\bW_t}$. This keeps storage at $R$ vectors per class,
revisits no past sample, and is empirically \emph{lossless} (an oracle that
refits past classes from raw rows gains $+0.05$).

\paragraph{The metric's regularisation is saturated.} Sweeping the shrinkage
$\delta$ in $\Sigma + \delta\,\tfrac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}{d}I$ over four
orders of magnitude moves the cone by $0.45$ (79.32--79.77); Ledoit--Wolf
independently selects $\delta{=}0.0094$. There are no points left in this knob.

\section{Eigen-augmented generators}

exp29 measures which geometric quantity predicts per-class error. Between-class
subspace overlap does not ($\rho{=}{+}0.037$ at rank 32; every trivial control,
including sample count, predicts better). \emph{Within}-class coverage does: the
mean own-subspace energy $\lVert P_c\bq\rVert$ of a class's held-out rows has
$\rho={-}0.585$ and strengthens with rank. Classes fail because their own test
rows fall outside their own training span---not because classes collide.

$k$-means centroids sit at centres of mass and span badly. We therefore augment
the generator set along the principal directions of within-class variation:
\begin{equation}
\bV_c = \widehat{\Big[\,
\mathbf{m}_1 \cdots \mathbf{m}_{n_k},\;
\bmu_c \pm \alpha\sqrt{\lambda_j}\,\bv_j \;(j<n_e)\Big]},
\quad n_k + 2n_e = R,
\end{equation}
with $(\bv_j,\lambda_j)$ the top eigenpairs of the within-class scatter in the
whitened space. Budget is held fixed so the ablation is not confounded with
generator count.

\midrule
$(4,0)$ centroids only & 4 & 79.77 & --- & --- \\

\bottomrule

\section{Composition with A\textsuperscript{+}}

A\textsuperscript{+} adapts LoRA ($1.77$M parameters, $2\%$ of the backbone) on
task 0 against a discarded linear head, freezes, and reads with RanPAC. It stores
\emph{no per-class covariance and no exemplars}---$8$\,MB of read-out weights
against the $473$\,MB of per-class covariance used by the compared methods.

The read-out and the backbone recipe are close to independent. A $3.75\times$
change in epoch budget \emph{and} a different LR schedule move ImageNet-A A-Last
by $0.06$; two separate mechanisms for limiting first-session drift---early
stopping at a target train accuracy, and a proximal anchor to the pre-trained
model---each \emph{cost} accuracy ($10/12$ cells worse; $-1.41$ at $\lambda{=}0.1$,
$-4.25$ at $\lambda{=}0.5$). Whatever first-session adaptation is doing, less of
it is not better.

\section{What does not work}

\paragraph{Lifting the cone.} RanPAC's random ReLU projection is worth $+9.26$
over NCM, larger than every other component combined, and every cone above
operates \emph{beneath} it. Moving generators and NNLS into $\mathrm{relu}(\phi P)$
gains nothing ($-0.07$ for the cone, $+0.20$ for max-cosine). The reason is
measurable: the lift \emph{doubles} mean pairwise cosine, $0.207 \to 0.429$, and
does not improve with $M$ ($0.4285$ at $M{=}2000$, $0.4289$ at $M{=}10000$).
Crowding every point into the non-negative orthant is harmless to a ridge, which
can build discriminative directions anywhere, and harmful to any reader that sees
only angles. \emph{The lift belongs to the ridge, not to the representation.}

\paragraph{The $H$-representation.} Replacing the generator description with a
facet description fixes the monotonicity of coverage in $R$ but fails three
pre-registered gates. The decisive one is the aggregation: min-over-facets
($L^\infty$) $<$ softmin $<$ sum-of-squares ($L^2$), monotone in \emph{how little
the rule resembles a conjunction}. Conic membership \emph{is} a conjunction, so
the conic reading is structurally locked into the worst pooling.

\paragraph{Fusion.} Combining the cone with RanPAC captures only $8$--$26\%$ of
the per-sample oracle headroom, and three structurally different combiners
(global $\beta$, per-sample gate, EMA-anchored $\beta$) land within $0.25$ of one
another. The channels are genuinely complementary---the fraction of samples the
cone gets right and RanPAC does not \emph{grows} $0.79 \to 3.75$ while their
correlation \emph{falls} $0.81\to0.50$---but that complementarity is not
extractable from the two score vectors.

\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{Seeds.} Table~\ref{tab:aplus} is three seeds. \emph{Every read-out
number in this paper is seed 0 only.} RanPAC alone spans $80.28/80.55/80.38$
across seeds on ImageNet-R $T{=}10$, which is the same order as several reported
differences. No read-out claim here should be treated as established until it is
seeded.

\paragraph{A solver defect.} Our NNLS implementation ran one of five hundred
FISTA iterations for its entire history: the convergence test compared a tensor
with itself. Every conic score produced before the fix was the one-step
approximation $\bw = \eta\,\mathrm{relu}(\bV\bq)$. On one cell the corrected
solver moves near-OOD AUROC from $0.62$ to $0.85$, and it reverses the sign of the
budget trend. We note this because the class of bug---a silent identity in a
convergence check---is not detectable from output that looks reasonable, and
because a single assertion catches it: at $k_{\text{local}}{=}1$ the conic score
must equal max-cosine \emph{exactly}.

\paragraph{Scope.} One backbone, four datasets, one adaptation recipe. The
geometric claims concern pre-trained ViT features in this regime and should not
be read as claims about conic parameterisations generally.
